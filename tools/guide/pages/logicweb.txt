# Logic Web
> Match everyone to everything with lines

## How to play

Every person and every item is a pill, one column per category. Tap a pill to choose it, then tap pills in another column to draw lines from it: one line means that one, and several lines mean one of those. Tap the same pill again to rule the pair out (a dashed line), and once more to clear it. Tap the chosen pill again, or the space between, to let it go.

What your marks imply is drawn faint. A pill with one line into a column is linked to that pill and ruled out for the rest of it, whatever is linked to one thing is linked to everything linked to it, a pair ruled out stays ruled out for everything linked to either end, and a pill with one choice left in a column is linked to it. You never need to draw a link the faint lines already show.

Choose a pill to see all its pairs: thick for linked, thin for one of its choices, dashed for ruled out, dotted for still open. Group lines every item up in its person's row, and the category buttons above the board show your marks as a table, with that category down the side and what is still possible in each open place. Tap a clue to strike it through once you've used it: it's only a note, to keep your place.

Nothing you mark is refused. A moment later, a pill with nothing left in some column, two of a kind linked together or a clue that can no longer be kept shows in red.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Each person has one item from every category: their house, and one or two more. No two people share an item.
- Every clue must be true.
- The houses stand in a row, numbered from the left. Next to means one house either side, and somewhere left of means a lower number.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 4 people, 1 thing each, clues name the person. Nothing needs more than a clue and what it leaves.
- Hard: 4 people, 2 things each, clues link things. Clues can also say who lives next to or left of whom.
- Expert: 5 people, 2 things each, either-or clues. Every board needs a what-if.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Try them in this order. Each board is the people's table, as the Person button shows it: a row for each person, and in each open place the items still possible, in small text; a larger word is linked. The tinted place is where to look, rings mark what the reasoning leans on, and the filled place is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### clue | A clue that names someone

A clue that names a person says something about them outright: draw their line to what it says they have, or rule out what it says they don't. If it says they have either of two things, draw a line to each. Start with these, because they need nothing else you have marked.

focus: r1c2
answer: r1c2=piano
caption: Clue 6 names Eve and says she plays the piano, so draw her line to it.
~~~

### only | The one item left

Something with one choice left in a column is that choice. On this board you never draw it yourself: the faint line appears as soon as everything else is ruled out, so the hints never need to point at it.

### link | Two things a clue ties together

A clue can tie two things to one person without saying who. As soon as you know who has one, they have the other, and whoever is ruled out for one is ruled out for the other. If a clue says two things belong to different people, whoever has one can't have the other.

~~~board
. piano .
. guitar white
1 . .
. . .
top: House Music Colour
left: Eve Kai Leo Raj
notes: r1c1=2,3 r1c3=red,yellow r2c1=2,3,4 r3c2=violin,harp r4c1=2,3,4 r4c2=violin,harp r4c3=red,green,yellow
focus: r3c3
cite: r3c1
answer: r3c3=green
caption: Clue 8 ties the green house to house 1. Leo, ringed, lives in house 1, so draw his line to the green house.
~~~

### house | Where the houses allow

Next to and somewhere left of rule houses out. Nobody lives left of house 1 or right of the last house, so those clues trim the ends straight away. Then read a clue against the houses people can still have: a person can't take a house with no possible partner on the right side of it.

~~~board
. piano .
. guitar white
. . .
. . .
top: House Music Colour
left: Eve Kai Leo Raj
notes: r1c1=2,3 r1c3=red,yellow r2c1=2,3,4 r3c2=violin,harp r3c3=red,green,yellow r4c1=1,2,3,4 r4c2=violin,harp r4c3=red,green,yellow
focus: r3c1
answer: r3c1=not_4
caption: Clue 4 puts Raj somewhere right of Leo. Nothing is right of house 4, so rule house 4 out for Leo.
~~~

### what-if | A what-if

Suppose a link, and follow what it forces, for a few steps at most. If someone ends up with nothing left, or a clue can't be kept, your supposition was wrong, so rule that pair out. This isn't guessing: you only mark it once it has been shown to break.
