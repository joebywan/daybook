# Reckon
> Make every cage's number, 1 to 6 once per line

## How to play

Tap a square, then tap a digit. Tap the digit again to clear it.

Or pick the digit first: with no square selected, tap a digit, then every square it goes in. Tap the selected square to let go of it, and the digit again to finish.

Tap the pencil to take notes. While it is lit, a digit is pencilled small in the square instead of placed, and placing a digit clears it from the notes of the squares in its row and column.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, 1 to 6 place a digit (or a note in pencil mode), and Backspace clears.

A repeat in a row or column, or a cage that can no longer make its number, turns red after a moment, but nothing stops you placing a digit.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". If a digit you placed is wrong, Hint points at that first. Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Fill every square with a digit from 1 to the size of the grid: no repeat in a row or a column.
- Bold lines mark cages. The number and sign in a cage's corner is what its digits make: added (+), multiplied (×), or in a cage of two, the bigger less (−) or over (÷) the smaller.
- A cage may repeat a digit when the two squares share no row or column.
- A cage of one square is given, and stays put.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: Cages of up to 3 squares, a few digits given. About 18 cages, 3 to 5 of them single given squares.
- Hard: Cages of up to 4 squares, one or two digits given. About 15 cages. Needs a cage that settles a digit for its whole line, or two squares sharing two digits, at least once.
- Expert: Cages of up to 5 squares, no digits given. About 13 cages, and the cages are all you start from. Needs a what-if at least once.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Try them in this order. On each board, the colours and bold lines are the cages, the darker tint is where to look, rings mark the digits the reasoning leans on, and the filled square is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### last-square | The last square in a row or column

A row or column with one empty square is missing one digit, so that digit goes there.

### cage-combination | A cage's digit combinations

Now the cages come in. List the ways a cage can make its number with the digits its squares can still take. Two squares making 3÷ are 1 and 3 or 2 and 6; 15× is only 3 and 5. A cage may repeat a digit, but only in squares that share no row or column, so its shape matters. When the ways leave a square one digit, that's it.

~~~board
6 . . . . 4
4 . 2 3 . 5
. . . . . .
. . . . . .
. . . . 5 .
. . . . . .
regions: abbccd aefghi jeklhm jjklhm nokpqm nopprr
corners: r1c1=24× r1c2=8+ r1c4=2÷ r2c2=2× r2c5=11+ r3c1=11+ r3c3=72× r3c4=24× r3c6=6+ r5c1=1− r5c2=10+ r5c4=10× r6c5=3−

cite: r2c2 r3c2

caption: 2× in two squares is only 1 and 2, either way round. Row 2 has its 2 already, so this square is the 1.
~~~

### naked-pair | Two squares sharing two digits

Two squares in a line that can each be only the same two digits take both of them, so no other square in that line can hold either. Strike them off and look for the single it leaves.

~~~board

. 1 2 . . .
. . . . . .
. . . . . .

. . . . . 6

caption: The ringed squares of row 2 can only be 3 or 5, so they take both. The 12+ cage's two squares in row 2 are then 4 and 6, which make 10, leaving 2 for its third.
~~~

### what-if | A what-if

Take a square left two digits and suppose one. Follow it a few steps: if a square, a line or a cage is left with nothing, the supposition was wrong and the square is the other digit.

~~~board
. . . . . .
. . . . . .
5 . . . . .
. . . . . .
. . 5 . . .
. . . . . .
regions: abccde abbcde ffbgee fhbgii jhhiik jhllik
corners: r1c1=2÷ r1c2=13+ r1c3=120× r1c5=11+ r1c6=48× r3c1=20× r3c4=2÷ r4c2=20+ r4c5=12+ r5c1=3− r5c6=2− r6c3=2×
focus: r1c3
cite: r6c3
answer: r1c3=6
caption: The 120× cage is 4 × 5 × 6 and column 3 has its 5, so this square is 4 or 6. Suppose 4: then row 6, column 3 would be 2, and column 2 would have nowhere for its 3. So it is 6.
~~~
