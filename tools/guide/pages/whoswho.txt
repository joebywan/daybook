# Who's Who
> Match everyone to everything from the clues

## How to play

Tap a square in the table, then an item under the board. With the tick chosen beside the items, that item is theirs; with the cross chosen, it isn't. The same tap again takes the mark off. Double-tap or long-press an item for the other mark.

A tick rules that item out of the rest of its column, and each open square shows what is still possible. Tap a clue to strike it through once you've used it: it's only a note, to keep your place.

On a keyboard, the arrows move between squares, 1 to 5 tick that item, Shift with 1 to 5 crosses it, and Backspace clears the square.

Nothing you mark is refused. A moment later, a square with nothing left, a tick two people share or a clue that can no longer be kept shows in red.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Each person has one item from every category: their house, and one or two more. No two people share an item.
- Every clue must be true.
- The houses stand in a row, numbered from the left. Next to means one house either side, and somewhere left of means a lower number.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 4 people, 1 thing each, clues name the person. Nothing needs more than a clue and what it leaves.
- Hard: 4 people, 2 things each, clues link things. Clues can also say who lives next to or left of whom.
- Expert: 5 people, 2 things each, either-or clues. Every board needs a what-if.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Try them in this order. On each board, the tinted square is where to look, rings mark what the reasoning leans on, and the filled square is the move. Small text is what a square can still hold, and a larger word is ticked. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

The first four boards are moments from one game, with these clues:

- 1. Eve lives somewhere left of the violinist.
- 2. Eve lives next to the green house.
- 3. Kai plays the guitar.
- 4. Leo lives somewhere left of Raj.
- 5. Eve lives next to the red house.
- 6. Eve plays the piano.
- 7. Kai lives in the white house.
- 8. The green house is house 1.

### clue | A clue that names someone

A clue that names a person says something about them outright: tick what it says they have, or cross what it says they don't. If it says they have either of two things, cross off everything else. Start with these, because they need nothing else on the table.

~~~board
. . .
. guitar .
. . .
. . .
top: House Music Colour
left: Eve Kai Leo Raj
notes: r1c1=1,2,3,4 r1c3=red,green,yellow,white r2c1=1,2,3,4 r2c3=red,green,yellow,white r3c1=1,2,3,4 r3c2=piano,violin,harp r3c3=red,green,yellow,white r4c1=1,2,3,4 r4c2=piano,violin,harp r4c3=red,green,yellow,white
focus: r1c2
answer: r1c2=piano
caption: Clue 6 names Eve and says she plays the piano, so tick it.
~~~

### only | The one item left

A square with one item left must be that item. It works down a column too: an item that only one person can still have is theirs.

~~~board
. piano .
. guitar white
. . .
. . .
top: House Music Colour
left: Eve Kai Leo Raj
notes: r1c1=2,3 r1c3=red,yellow r2c1=2,3,4 r3c2=violin,harp r3c3=red,green,yellow r4c1=2,3,4 r4c2=violin,harp r4c3=red,green,yellow
focus: r3c1
answer: r3c1=1
caption: Look down the House column. Nobody else's square offers house 1 any more, so it's Leo's.
~~~

### link | Two things a clue ties together

A clue can tie two things to one person without saying who. As soon as you know who has one, they have the other, and whoever is ruled out for one is ruled out for the other. If a clue says two things belong to different people, whoever has one can't have the other.

~~~board
. piano .
. guitar white
1 . .
. . .
top: House Music Colour
left: Eve Kai Leo Raj
notes: r1c1=2,3 r1c3=red,yellow r2c1=2,3,4 r3c2=violin,harp r4c1=2,3,4 r4c2=violin,harp r4c3=red,green,yellow
focus: r3c3
cite: r3c1
answer: r3c3=green
caption: Clue 8 ties the green house to house 1. Leo, ringed, lives in house 1, so the green house is his.
~~~

### house | Where the houses allow

Next to and somewhere left of rule houses out. Nobody lives left of house 1 or right of the last house, so those clues trim the ends straight away. Then read a clue against the houses people can still have: a person can't take a house with no possible partner on the right side of it.

~~~board
. piano .
. guitar white
. . .
. . .
top: House Music Colour
left: Eve Kai Leo Raj
notes: r1c1=2,3 r1c3=red,yellow r2c1=2,3,4 r3c2=violin,harp r3c3=red,green,yellow r4c1=1,2,3,4 r4c2=violin,harp r4c3=red,green,yellow
focus: r3c1
answer: r3c1=not_4
caption: Clue 4 puts Raj somewhere right of Leo. Nothing is right of house 4, so cross house 4 off for Leo.
~~~

### what-if | A what-if

Suppose a tick, and follow what it forces, for a few steps at most. If someone ends up with nothing left, or a clue can't be kept, your supposition was wrong, so put a cross there. This isn't guessing: you only mark it once it has been shown to break.
