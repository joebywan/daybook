# Tally
> Keep the numbers that make every sum

## How to play

Pick Remove or Keep under the board and tap numbers; double-tap or long-press for the other. Drag along a row or column to sweep several at once.

Keep circles a number you are sure of. It is only a note: a circled number counts exactly like any other left in.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, X removes, K circles, Space cycles, and Backspace clears. Shift and an arrow carries a mark along.

A target turns green when its line adds up. It turns red a moment after no choice of the numbers still open can make it.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". If a mark you made is wrong, Hint points at that first. Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- The number beside each row, and under each column, is its target.
- Remove numbers so that what is left in every row and every column adds up to its target.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 5x5 grid. The first four techniques below are all it needs.
- Hard: 6x6 grid. Needs the ways a line can make its target at least once.
- Expert: 7x7 grid. Needs a what-if at least once.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Try them in this order. On each board, the targets are the coloured squares at the edge, the tint is the line to look at, a ring is a number already circled, a red cross is a number already removed, and the filled square is the number to mark next. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### line-done | A line already making its target

If the circled numbers in a line already make its target, nothing else there can stay.

~~~board
1 1 4 6 9 13
7 9 3 8 4 24
7 2 4 2 2 8
6 4 1 4 3 9
1 8 4 8 2 10
13 11 8 16 16 _
regions: .....a .....a .....a .....a .....a aaaaa.
focus: r4c1-r4c6
cite: r1c3 r1c5 r2c2 r2c4 r4c1 r4c5
struck: r1c1=x r1c2=x r1c4=x r4c2=x r4c4=x r5c2=x
answer: r4c3=1
caption: In row 4 the circled 6 and 3 already make 9, its target. So the 1 goes.
~~~

### too-big | A number too big for its line

Take what a line still needs: its target, less anything circled. A number bigger than that can't stay.

~~~board
3 4 2 2 7 14
3 3 8 5 7 12
6 8 5 8 9 14
3 1 7 2 7 11
4 1 3 2 1 9
16 14 3 5 22 _
regions: .....a .....a .....a .....a .....a aaaaa.
focus: r1c4-r6c4
struck: r2c3=x r3c3=x r4c3=x
answer: r3c4=8
caption: Column 4's target is 5, so its 8 is too big to stay. Remove it.
~~~

### too-much | A number the line can't lose

Add up what is left in a line and see how far over its target it is. A number bigger than that surplus has to stay, because removing it would take away too much.

~~~board
3 4 2 2 7 14
3 3 8 5 7 12
6 8 5 8 9 14
3 1 7 2 7 11
4 1 3 2 1 9
16 14 3 5 22 _
regions: .....a .....a .....a .....a .....a aaaaa.
focus: r1c1-r1c6
struck: r2c3=x r3c3=x r3c4=x r4c3=x
answer: r1c5=7
caption: Row 1 adds up to 18, which is 4 over its target of 14. Removing the 7 would take away too much, so it stays. Circle it.
~~~

### all-left | A line where everything left stays

If what is left in a line makes its target exactly, all of it stays. Such a line has already turned green, so this is tidying up.

~~~board
3 4 2 2 7 3 13
3 8 5 7 6 8 26
5 8 9 3 1 7 20
2 7 4 1 3 2 6
1 1 1 3 9 1 13
4 2 7 3 7 6 10
5 8 26 16 22 11 _
regions: ......a ......a ......a ......a ......a ......a aaaaaa.
focus: r1c3-r7c3
cite: r1c5 r2c3 r2c4 r2c5 r3c3 r4c3 r5c5 r6c3 r6c4
struck: r1c3=x r3c5=x r4c2=x r4c5=x r6c1=x r6c2=x r6c5=x r6c6=x
answer: r5c3=1
caption: With its 2 gone, column 3 adds up to exactly 26, its target, so everything left stays. The 1 is the last to circle.
~~~

### every-way | The ways a line can make its target

When the shortcuts run out, list the ways a line can still make its target. A number in every way stays, and a number in none goes.

~~~board
3 4 2 2 7 3 13
3 8 5 7 6 8 26
5 8 9 3 1 7 20
2 7 4 1 3 2 6
1 1 1 3 9 1 13
4 2 7 3 7 6 10
5 8 26 16 22 11 _
regions: ......a ......a ......a ......a ......a ......a aaaaaa.
focus: r2c1-r2c7
cite: r1c5 r2c3 r2c4 r2c5 r3c3 r4c3 r5c3 r5c5 r6c3 r6c4
struck: r1c3=x r3c5=x r4c2=x r4c5=x r6c1=x r6c2=x r6c5=x r6c6=x
answer: r2c1=3
caption: The circled numbers in row 2 make 18, so it needs 8 more from an open 3, 8 and 8. Either 8 does it, and neither way uses the 3, so the 3 goes even before you know which 8 stays.
~~~

### what-if | A what-if

Suppose a number stays, or goes, and follow what that forces through a few lines. If some line is left with no way to make its target, your supposition was wrong, so the number does the opposite. This isn't guessing: you only mark it once the other way has been shown to break.

~~~board
1 6 1 1 5 7 4 19
6 2 1 1 5 8 3 10
6 3 1 3 6 4 8 24
2 3 9 5 3 8 1 15
8 5 6 5 3 9 6 28
5 6 2 2 1 3 8 17
2 8 7 7 7 9 2 9
28 14 18 13 15 19 15 _
regions: .......a .......a .......a .......a .......a .......a .......a aaaaaaa.
focus: r2c3
cite: r1c1 r1c5 r1c6 r2c1 r3c1 r3c3 r3c5 r3c7 r4c3 r5c1 r6c1 r6c2 r6c5 r6c6
struck: r2c5=x r2c6=x r3c6=x r4c6=x r6c7=x r7c2=x r7c5=x
answer: r2c3=1
caption: If this 1 went, row 2 would keep its other 1 and lose its 2, then column 2 would lose its 6, then row 1 would keep the 1 in column 4. Column 4 would then hold two 1s and need 11 more from 3, 5, 5, 2 and 7, which nothing makes, so this 1 stays.
~~~
