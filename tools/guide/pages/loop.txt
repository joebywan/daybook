# Loop
> One loop round the numbers

## How to play

Drag from dot to dot to draw the line along the sides of the squares. Drag back along a line to rub it out. Tap an edge between two dots to cross it out: a cross is only a note, for an edge you know the loop doesn't use.

On a keyboard, the arrows move between dots, Shift and an arrow draws or rubs out the line that way, and X then an arrow crosses that edge.

After a moment, red marks what can't stand: a number with too many lines round it, a dot with three lines, or a closed loop that leaves something out. Nothing stops you drawing it. A number with all its lines drawn turns lighter, so what is left stands out.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". Every board has exactly one loop, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Draw one closed loop along the sides of the squares, from dot to dot. It never crosses or branches.
- A number says how many of that square's four sides the loop runs along. Squares without a number can have any.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 5x5 grid. The numbers' and dots' own rules finish it.
- Hard: 7x7 grid. Needs the rule that there's only one loop.
- Expert: 10x10 grid. Needs a what-if.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. On each board, the tinted square is where to look, grey lines and crosses are already drawn, and the coloured line or cross is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### count | A number's count

A number with all its lines already drawn has no more: its other sides are crossed. A number with only as many sides left as it needs takes all of them. A 0 crosses all four sides at once.

~~~board
1 0 . 1 0
. . . . 1
. 3 2 . 2
. . . . 2
2 1 1 . .
draw: dots
focus: r1c2
moves: r1c2xr1c3 r1c3xr2c3 r2c2xr2c3 r1c2xr2c2
caption: A 0 has no line round it, so all four of its sides are crossed.
~~~

### one-way | A line with one way on

A line can't stop. A dot with one line into it and only one way left sends the line on that way.

~~~board
1 0 . 1 0
. . . . 1
. 3 2 . 2
. . . . 2
2 1 1 . .
draw: dots
lines: r1c1xr1c2 r1c2xr1c3 r1c5xr1c6 r2c1-r2c2 r2c2xr2c3 r2c5xr2c6 r1c1xr2c1 r1c2xr2c2 r1c3xr2c3 r1c5xr2c5 r1c6xr2c6
moves: r2c1-r3c1
caption: The line along the top of row 2 has reached the left edge of the board. The edge above is crossed, so its only way on is down.
~~~

### dead-end | A dead end

A dot with no line yet and only one way left can never be passed through: the loop would need two ways. So that way is crossed.

~~~board
1 0 . 1 0
. . . . 1
. 3 2 . 2
. . . . 2
2 1 1 . .
draw: dots
lines: r1c2xr1c3 r1c5xr1c6 r2c2xr2c3 r2c5xr2c6 r1c2xr2c2 r1c3xr2c3 r1c5xr2c5 r1c6xr2c6
moves: r1c1xr1c2
caption: The 0 has crossed two of the edges at the dot between the 1 and the 0. With one way left, the loop can never pass through it, so that way is crossed too.
~~~

### corner | A number and its corners

Look at a number together with the dots at its four corners. Each dot takes no line or two, never one. Often only one way of giving the number its count keeps every corner right, and an edge is settled. A 3 in a corner of the board always takes both sides at that corner; a 1 there takes neither.

~~~board
1 0 . 3 .
. . 2 . 1
. . 0 2 .
1 . . . .
. 0 . . .
draw: dots
focus: r1c4
lines: r1c1xr1c2 r1c2xr1c3 r1c3xr1c4 r2c1-r2c2 r2c2xr2c3 r3c3xr3c4 r4c3xr4c4 r5c2xr5c3 r6c1xr6c2 r6c2xr6c3 r6c3xr6c4 r1c1xr2c1 r1c2xr2c2 r1c3xr2c3 r2c1-r3c1 r2c2-r3c2 r3c3xr4c3 r3c4xr4c4 r5c1xr6c1 r5c2xr6c2 r5c3xr6c3
moves: r1c4-r1c5 r1c4-r2c4
caption: The dot at this 3's top-left corner has no way out except along the 3's top and left sides, so both are lines or neither is. A 3 leaves out only one side, so both are lines.
~~~
