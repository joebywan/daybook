# Links
> Join every pair, fill every square

## How to play

Drag from a number, or from the end of a line, to draw a path through the squares. A line can also start on any empty square; it takes a pair's colour once it reaches one. Drag back along a line to rub it out, and go back over your own drag to take back the last squares. Drawing across another pair's path cuts it there.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, and Shift and an arrow draws from the square you are on (or rubs out the line that way).

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Join each pair of matching numbers with one path through squares that share a side.
- Paths never cross or touch the same square, and every square is on a path.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 6x6 grid, 5 to 7 pairs. A line's end with one way on, and a square with two ways in, finish it.
- Hard: 8x8 grid, 7 to 9 pairs. Needs what a way would cut off.
- Expert: 10x10 grid, 9 to 12 pairs. Needs what a way would cut off, or a what-if.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. On each board, the tinted square is where to look, grey lines are already drawn, and the coloured line is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### one-way | A line's end with one way on

A line can't stop short of its pair. When the end of a line, or a number with no line yet, has only one square it can step to, the line goes there.

~~~board
1 . . . . .
. 2 . . 3 .
2 4 3 4 5 .
6 . . . . .
. 5 . . . 1
. . . . . 6
draw: discs
focus: r3c1
moves: r3c1-r2c1
caption: This 2 has a 4 to its right and a 6 below it, so its only way on is up.
~~~

### two-ways | A square with two ways in

Every square is on a path, and a path through a square uses two of its sides. When only two neighbours could join an empty square, it joins both.

### cut-off | A way that would cut a path off

Look at each way an end or a square could go. If taking one would cut a pair off from its other end, or leave a square nothing can fill, it can't go that way.

### what-if | A what-if

An end with two ways: suppose one, and follow the moves it forces a few steps. If something breaks, such as a pair cut off or a square boxed in, the end takes the other way. This isn't guessing: you only draw the line once the other choice has been shown to break.

~~~board
1 . . . . . . . . 2
. . 3 . . . . . 4 .
. . . . . . . . 5 .
. . . . . . . . . .
. . . . 6 . . 3 . .
. . . . . . . . . .
. . . 7 6 . . . . .
. . . . . 7 8 . . 9
. . . 2 . 8 5 . 4 .
. . 1 9 . . . . . .
draw: discs
focus: r10c8
lines: r7c7-r8c7 r9c1-r10c1 r9c10-r10c10 r10c1-r10c2 r10c4-r10c5 r10c8-r10c9 r10c9-r10c10
moves: r10c7-r10c8
caption: Suppose this loose line went up from the bottom row. A few forced moves later, path 5 would be cut off from its other end. So it goes left.
~~~
